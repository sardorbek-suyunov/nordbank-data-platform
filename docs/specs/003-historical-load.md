# 003 — Deterministic Historical Load

Status: Approved
Depends on: 000, 001, 002

## Goal
A deterministic generator that populates the sixteen `core` tables with a
coherent, statistically plausible operating history for the simulated bank, at
three scales, loaded by `COPY`, verifiable by hash and by invariant. This
completes M2.

## Determinism contract

Three inputs fully determine the output: `NORDBANK_SEED` (integer),
`NORDBANK_ANCHOR_DATE` (date, the simulated "today" at the end of the load, and
defaulting to the real current date), and `NORDBANK_ENV` (`ci`, `dev`, `full`).

Same three inputs, byte-identical database. This means:

- One seeded random source, passed explicitly. No module-level `random`, no
  `numpy.random` global state, no `uuid4`, no `time`, no `now()` in generated
  values, no reliance on dictionary or set iteration order, no parallelism that
  affects value assignment.
- Every generated timestamp derives from the anchor date and the seed, never
  from the clock.
- Verified by hashing a canonically ordered dump of every table and comparing
  across two runs from a nuked stack.

**`created_at` and `updated_at` are set explicitly by the loader.** The
`set_updated_at` trigger fires `before update` only, so an insert does not
touch it. The constraint inherited from spec 002 is refined here: no code path
may set `updated_at` on an `UPDATE`, and every `INSERT` must set it to the
row's true last-change time in simulated history. A historical row whose
`updated_at` is the load timestamp is a defect — it would make the entire
history arrive in one watermark window and leave M4 with nothing to
demonstrate. `updated_at >= created_at` and both are `<= anchor_date` for every
row.

## Scope

### 1. Profiles

| Profile | Customers | History | Target transactions | Seed runtime |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ci` | ~500 | 6 months | tens of thousands | under 20 s |
| `dev` | ~5,000 | 3 years | 2–4 million | under 5 min |
| `full` | ~250,000 | 5 years | tens of millions | measured, no target |

Profiles differ only in parameters. No generator code branches on the profile
except to read those parameters. If a distribution only works at one scale that
is a defect.

Reconcile the measured outcome against the figures in `architecture.md` and
correct that document if reality differs. Do not adjust the generator to
protect a number in a document.

### 2. Package structure
`generator/` as an importable package with a clear separation between deciding
and writing:

- `config.py` — profile parameters, loaded from a committed
  `generator/profiles.yml`, validated on load. Every tunable number lives here,
  not inline in generator code.
- `rng.py` — the seeded random source and derived sub-streams, so that adding a
  new entity generator does not shift the values produced by existing ones.
  This is a hard requirement: a change to loan generation must not alter a
  single transaction.
- `entities/` — one module per entity or closely coupled group, each producing
  rows as plain records, with no database access.
- `realism/` — the distribution and behaviour models: seasonality, amount
  distributions, fraud propensity, delinquency, churn, acquisition.
- `writer.py` — the `COPY` loader, dependency ordering, sequence
  synchronisation, transaction and chunking policy.
- `invariants.py` — the coherence checks, runnable independently of a load.
- `manifest.py` — the run manifest.
- `__main__.py` — CLI: `python -m generator --profile dev --seed 42
  --anchor 2026-09-18`.

### 3. Bulk loading

**Probe before you design.** `bigint generated always as identity` may refuse
explicit values under `COPY`. Establish empirically what PostgreSQL 16 does,
then choose:
- If `COPY` accepts explicit identity values, use it and synchronise every
  sequence with `setval` afterwards.
- If it refuses, my lean is changing the primary keys to `generated by default
  as identity`, which is the standard way to say the loader may supply keys,
  costing only the guarantee that nobody supplies one accidentally. Staging
  tables plus `INSERT ... OVERRIDING SYSTEM VALUE` is the alternative; argue for
  it if you prefer it.

Report the measurement and your recommendation before implementing.

Also measure and document: whether foreign key validation during load is
affordable at `full` scale with the indexes from design rule 12, whether
dropping and recreating secondary indexes around the load is worth it, and what
`COPY` chunk size the GL constraint trigger's memory bound permits. The 100k
entry-row figure from spec 002 is the starting point, not a conclusion.

Rows are loaded in dependency order, parents before children. `ref` and
`platform` are not touched; they are already seeded.

### 4. Realism specification
Deliver `docs/generator_realism.md`: every distribution, its parameters, the
justification, and an explicit list of what is deliberately unrealistic and
why. A number with no stated reason is a defect.

At minimum:

**Volume and timing.** Transaction arrival with hour-of-day bimodality, a
weekday to weekend shift in category mix, monthly seasonality with a December
peak and an August trough, and a growth trend across the history so that
cohorts differ in size and Q1's growth measure is not flat noise.

**Amounts.** Log-normal per MCC category with category-specific parameters.
Cash withdrawals cluster on multiples of 10, 20 and 50. Subscription and
recurring payments repeat the same amount on the same day of month. The
resulting first-digit distribution of transaction amounts should approximate
Benford's law; this is a testable property and becomes an invariant check.

**Card presentment.** An explicit `is_card_present` decision per card
transaction, with the card-not-present share rising across the history. Channel
does not determine presentment: a mobile wallet tap at a terminal is card
present. This is why the column exists rather than being derived.

**Currency and geography.** Predominantly EUR with a minority of
multi-currency accounts, a cross-border share by corridor, and a merchant
country distribution consistent with the customer country mix.

**Fraud.** Between 0.05 and 0.15 percent of card transactions are fraudulent,
concentrated in card-not-present, unfamiliar merchants, unusual hours and
velocity bursts. Detection is imperfect in both directions: alerts fire on some
legitimate transactions and miss some fraud, so that Q10's precision lands in a
plausible band rather than at 100 percent. State the target precision and
recall and hit them.

**Lending.** Applications with an approval rate that varies by risk band,
a disbursement lag, a scheduled installment plan consistent with principal,
rate and term, and repayment behaviour producing on-time payment, late payment,
cure and default. Default probability varies by risk band and delinquency
emerges over the months following origination rather than immediately, so that
Q7's vintage curves have shape.

**Attrition and dormancy.** Accounts close and customers go dormant at rates
that make Q2's retention curve decay rather than sit flat.

**Digital behaviour.** Login sessions per active customer with a stable device
fingerprint per customer plus occasional new devices, correlated with the fraud
model so that Q19 measures a real signal rather than noise.

**Sanctions positives.** A configurable small fraction of payment
counterparty names is drawn from a committed fixture,
`generator/fixtures/screening_positives.csv`, containing unmistakably synthetic
names. M4 injects the same fixture into the local sanctions snapshot so that
screening has something to match. **No real sanctioned individual's name
appears anywhere in this repository**, and the reason is stated in the realism
document: a simulated bank does not need real people's names to demonstrate
screening, and putting them in a portfolio repository would be indefensible.

### 5. Coherence invariants
`generator/invariants.py`, runnable as `make seed-verify` against a loaded
database, every check reporting pass or fail with a count and an example
offender. All must pass:

1. No transaction, payment or card event before the account opened or after it
   closed.
2. No card transaction before the card was issued or after it expired, and none
   on a card whose status forbids authorisation at that time.
3. `account_holders.ownership_weight` sums to exactly 1 per account, and every
   account has at least one holder with `is_primary`.
4. `accounts.balance` equals the signed sum of posted transactions and payments
   for that account. This is the invariant most likely to be violated by a
   generator that produces transactions and balances independently.
5. Total debits equal total credits per posting date per currency, and every GL
   batch balances — the trigger guarantees the batch, this checks the day.
6. Every monetary event in `core` has corresponding GL entries. A transaction
   with no ledger impact is a defect.
7. Installment schedules are consistent with the loan's principal, rate and
   term; `paid_amount <= due_amount`; no payment before the loan was disbursed.
8. Every loan traces to an approved application; every disbursed loan's amount
   is within its approved amount.
9. Every fraud alert references an existing transaction, and the confirmed-fraud
   rate among alerts falls within the band stated in the realism document.
10. Every customer is at least 18 years old at their first account opening.
11. `updated_at >= created_at` for every row, and both `<= anchor_date`.
12. No orphan in any foreign key. Enforced by the database, asserted here so
    that a future load with constraints deferred cannot slip past.
13. Every active customer has at least one login session in the trailing 90
    days before the anchor.
14. Transaction amount first digits approximate Benford's law within a stated
    tolerance.

### 6. Manifest
Each run writes `generator/manifests/<profile>.json`: seed, anchor date,
profile, generator version, per-table row counts, per-table content hash, and
total runtime. **The `ci` manifest is committed**, and a CI step regenerates it
and fails on any difference. That makes determinism an enforced invariant
rather than a claim. `dev` and `full` manifests are gitignored.

### 7. Interchange carried forward from M2
- Add `is_card_present` to `ref.interchange_rates` and extend its key.
- Seed the four inter-regional rates from the 2019 commitments, described as
  the last published binding caps with their expiry date and the case
  references, not as current regulation.
- Add `is_card_present` to `core.transactions`.
- Commercial card rates remain null and fail loudly at M6.
- Update the data dictionary, the classifications, `metric_definitions.md` and
  the ERD accordingly.

### 8. Forward compatibility
Do not build these, but do not preclude them:
- The mutation engine at M3 advances the source by one business day. The
  loader's output must be a valid starting state for that, and the runbook must
  document the pattern M4 needs: load with an anchor in the past, then step
  forward, so that a backfill has genuine day-by-day change to extract.
- Card settlement files at M4 are derived from transactions plus the scheme's
  settlement lag, with deliberately injected breaks. Transactions must carry
  enough to derive a settlement file without reimplementing business logic.

### 9. Make targets
- `seed` — generate and load, honouring the three environment variables,
  reporting per-table row counts and elapsed time.
- `seed-verify` — run the invariants against the loaded database.
- `seed-manifest` — write the manifest; with `CHECK=1`, compare against the
  committed one and fail on difference.
`seed-verify` is wired into `make test-integration`.

### 10. Tests
- Unit tests for the realism models with a fixed seed: distribution shapes,
  seasonality, the presentment trend, the delinquency curve, sub-stream
  independence. Assert on statistics, not on individual values.
- A test proving sub-stream independence: adding a draw to one entity's stream
  does not change another's output.
- Invariant checks unit-tested against deliberately broken fixtures, so a
  check that cannot fail is caught.
- Integration test loading the `ci` profile, running every invariant, and
  comparing the manifest.

### 11. Continuous integration
`stack.yml` gains, after `schema-apply`: seed the `ci` profile, run
`seed-verify`, and run `seed-manifest CHECK=1`. Report the added runtime; if it
pushes the workflow beyond roughly eight minutes, propose how to keep it
bounded rather than silently accepting the growth.

## Out of scope
The mutation engine, extraction, contracts, dbt, settlement file generation,
the sanctions feed, anything in the warehouse.

## Acceptance criteria
1. `make seed` on a nuked, schema-applied stack loads all sixteen `core` tables
   with row counts inside the profile's stated band.
2. Two runs with identical seed, anchor and profile produce identical per-table
   hashes. Prove it from a nuked stack, twice.
3. Changing only the seed changes the data; changing only the anchor shifts the
   history; changing only the profile changes the scale. Demonstrate all three.
4. No generated row has `updated_at` or `created_at` equal to the load time.
   Show the distribution of `updated_at` across the history.
5. All fourteen invariants pass on `ci` and on `dev`. Report each with its
   count.
6. Every invariant is proven capable of failing, by breaking a fixture.
7. The Benford check passes on `dev`, and the first-digit distribution is
   reported alongside the expected one.
8. Fraud rate, alert precision and recall, approval rate by risk band, and
   default rate by risk band all fall within the bands stated in
   `docs/generator_realism.md`. Report measured against stated.
9. `accounts.balance` reconciles to transaction history for every account, with
   zero exceptions.
10. Total debits equal total credits for every posting date and currency.
11. Sub-stream independence is demonstrated: a change to one entity generator
    leaves another entity's hash unchanged.
12. `ci` seed completes within 20 seconds; `dev` within 5 minutes; `full`
    completes and its runtime is documented.
13. The committed `ci` manifest matches a fresh regeneration, and CI enforces
    it.
14. No real sanctioned individual's name appears in the repository; the fixture
    is demonstrably synthetic.
15. `docs/generator_realism.md` justifies every parameter and lists what is
    deliberately unrealistic.
16. All existing checks pass; delivered as a pull request on
    `feat/M2-historical-load` with all four required checks green.

## Amendments

Appended during implementation. The scope text above is left as issued; the protocol is in
`docs/specs/README.md`.

### 2026-09-18 — The identity probe resolves in favour of the existing primary keys

Section 3 anticipated that `COPY` might refuse explicit values into a
`bigint generated always as identity` column, and leaned towards relaxing the primary keys to
`generated by default as identity`.

Measured on PostgreSQL 16.15: `COPY` accepts them. A plain `INSERT` of the same explicit value
is still refused with `cannot insert a non-DEFAULT value into column`, so `generated always`
keeps exactly the guarantee spec 002 design rule 6 was written for while the loader supplies
every key. Relaxing the keys would have surrendered that guarantee to buy a capability `COPY`
already has. Design rule 6 is unamended and no primary key changes.

Two consequences are load-bearing rather than incidental. `COPY` does not advance the identity
sequence, so `setval` after every table is mandatory and its omission is invisible until the
M3 mutation engine's first insert collides. And a NULL in the identity column under `COPY` is
a not-null violation rather than a fallback to the default, so there is no mixed mode: the
generator assigns every key of every row. Recorded in ADR 0011.

### 2026-09-18 — `ref.payment_statuses` gains `is_posted`

Invariant 4 reconciles `accounts.balance` against "posted transactions and payments".
`ref.transaction_statuses.is_posted` supplies the rule for the transaction side. The payment
side had no equivalent: `ref.payment_statuses` carries `is_declined` and `is_final` and
nothing that says whether the account moved.

Rather than settle the rule in Python and share a constant between the generator and the
invariant, the column is added to the reference table. A shared Python constant would be a
second source of truth that dbt cannot read at M5, which is the failure mode design rule 7
exists to prevent. The column passes limb one of that rule with invariant 4 and the deposit
balance metric as named consumers. The generator and the invariant both read it from the
database.

### 2026-09-18 — The general ledger carries a source reference

Invariant 6 requires every monetary event in `core` to have corresponding GL entries.
`core.gl_entries` carried no column pointing at the transaction or payment that caused the
posting, so the invariant had no join path and was not checkable as written.

The reference goes on `core.gl_transactions`, not on `core.gl_entries`: a business event
produces a batch, and the entries are that batch's lines. It is implemented as
`source_entity_code` referencing a new `ref.gl_source_entities` lookup, plus
`source_entity_id bigint`, with a check that both are null or both are set, and a composite
index on the pair. `ref.gl_source_entities` earns a table on design rule 7's open-vocabulary
limb: source event types grow as the bank models more postings.

**The trade-off, stated rather than discovered.** A polymorphic reference cannot carry a
foreign key, so referential integrity is lost at that join and is replaced by invariant 6 here
and a data quality test at M7. Real core banking ledgers are polymorphic downstream of
everything for this reason, and four nullable foreign keys would not survive the next posting
type.

### 2026-09-18 — No inter-regional interchange rate is seeded, and the rate table gains no presentment dimension

Section 7 required seeding the four 2019 inter-regional caps and adding `is_card_present` to
`ref.interchange_rates`. Working the corridor through invalidates both.

European Commission press release IP/19/2311, cases AT.40049 and AT.39398, caps interchange on
cards **issued outside the EEA and used at EEA merchants** — the inbound corridor. Nordbank is
an EEA issuer, so it never earns that interchange. Nordbank's cards at non-EEA merchants are
the outbound inter-regional corridor, which those commitments do not govern and for which no
public figure exists. Intra-EEA is already correctly seeded under the Interchange Fee
Regulation caps, which do not vary by presentment.

So the four figures have no correct row to land on. Seeding them against the non-EEA merchant
regions would attach a real, citable number to the wrong corridor, which is the false
precision the null rates exist to prevent. **Outbound inter-regional rates stay null beside
the commercial rates, and Q4 fails loudly for both.**

`is_card_present` is therefore removed from `ref.interchange_rates` and from section 7: its
only justification was a presentment split governing a corridor Nordbank is not in, and a
dimension no rate varies by is ceremony. `is_card_present` stays on `core.transactions`, which
was always the stronger half of the change and is justified on its own terms — fraud
concentrates in card-not-present, and Q10 and Q19 depend on it.

The analysis is recorded in `metric_definitions.md` under the interchange entry. Because Q4 is
now answerable only for intra-EEA volume, the non-EEA merchant share is held to a few percent
so the null exposure is bounded, and the figure is stated in `docs/generator_realism.md`.

### 2026-09-18 — Invariant 9's statistical band is suspended at `ci` rather than widened

At `ci` scale a fraud rate of 0.05 to 0.15 percent yields a few dozen fraudulent transactions,
and precision and recall over that many events have a binomial confidence interval wider than
any band worth stating. A band wide enough to be honest at that n asserts nothing, which is a
fake pass wearing a confidence interval.

At `ci`, invariant 9 asserts structural properties only: alerts exist, every alert references
a valid transaction, and dispositions are drawn from the reference vocabulary. It reports the
statistical band as not asserted at this scale, printing the n and the interval width that
justify the suspension. The band is asserted at `dev` and `full`.

Acceptance criterion 5 is amended accordingly: it reads "all fourteen invariants pass on `ci`
and on `dev`, with invariant 9's statistical band suspended at `ci` and the suspension
reported", so that the criterion cannot later be read as fourteen unqualified green ticks.

### 2026-09-18 — Invariant 14 asserts Benford on card purchases only

Section 4 requires log-normal amounts, cash withdrawals clustered on multiples of 10, 20 and
50, and recurring payments repeating an identical amount, and then requires the composite
first-digit distribution to approximate Benford's law. Round-number clustering and repeated
identical amounts are anti-Benford by construction.

The amount model wins and the Benford assertion narrows. Tuning sigmas and mix weights to pass
a statistical test would trade a property a reviewer recognises as real for a demonstration.

- Invariant 14 asserts Benford on the population where it is theoretically expected: card
  purchase amounts, excluding cash withdrawals and recurring payments. The tolerance is a
  chi-square critical value at 8 degrees of freedom, so the check is honest at every scale
  rather than vacuous at one and flaky at another.
- The composite first-digit distribution over all transaction amounts is measured and reported
  alongside as an observation rather than a gate, with the deviation attributed to the
  excluded populations.

The coupling and this resolution are recorded in `docs/generator_realism.md`.

### 2026-09-18 — Invariant 13 is replaced by a login-to-transaction coverage share

Invariant 13 as issued required every active customer to have a login session in the trailing
90 days. A real bank fails that daily: dormancy is precisely the state of being active and not
logging in, and section 4 requires dormancy to be real enough that Q2's retention curve
decays. Satisfying the invariant would have meant constructing an unrealism to pass a check.

The invariant's purpose is to guarantee Q19 is answerable, which it did not express. It is
replaced by:

> A stated minimum share of customer-initiated transactions on digital channels is preceded by
> a login session for the same customer within the preceding 24 hours, and the share is
> reported. Recurring and card-present transactions are excluded, since neither implies a
> login.

The share is set in `generator/profiles.yml` and justified in `docs/generator_realism.md`.
